# Format direction

## Characters

- Preserve facial structure, proportions, hairstyle, eye color, skin tone, costume construction, equipment, signature motifs, and silhouette across generations and views.
- Favor poised expressions and anatomically credible poses.
- Use anime softness inside firm graphic structure; do not flatten a specific character into a generic archetype.
- Keep clothing and props mechanically coherent. When evidence is uncertain, preserve what is visible rather than inventing identity-changing details.

## Sticker sheets

- Build a modular, iconic, reusable family rather than a loose collage.
- Mix one clear hero subject with supporting objects, tools, symbols, landscapes, labels, film or technical elements, and small texture moments.
- Use consistent cutlines, mode-appropriate backing (cream in light mode; charcoal with cream cutlines in dark mode), controlled corner radii, visual rhythm, and generous breathing room.
- Vary scale and shape while keeping one grid and one palette.
- Make every sticker legible alone and coherent as part of the sheet.

## Character sheets

- Make them clean, information-rich, and production-ready.
- Include a strong hero view plus the useful subset of head angles, expression or detail crops, front/side/back turnaround, silhouette, palette chips, costume construction, props, and weapons.
- Prioritize identity consistency and usable reference information over decoration.
- Keep anatomy, costume, and equipment consistent across every panel.

## Full illustrations

- Create one cinematic, atmospheric, cohesive image with a decisive focal point and readable depth.
- Let the system shape the crop, geometry, palette, negative space, and subtle marks; do not turn the scene into a sticker sheet.
- Use typography only when it serves the composition, secondary to the image.
- Favor restrained light, analogue texture, human scale, architecture, landscape, filmic moments, and emotional stillness over spectacle.

## Other formats

For editorial graphics, photographic pieces, and technical visuals, preserve the same palette, grid, hierarchy, negative space, texture, and severity. Translate the grammar to the medium instead of repeating motifs literally.
