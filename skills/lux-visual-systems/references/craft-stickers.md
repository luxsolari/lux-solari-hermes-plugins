## Choose references for the brief

After the rendering-language and color-mode gates, scan the relevant category
below. Resolve paths relative to the installed skill directory; every entry is
packaged, including those mapped to the older boards. Visually inspect candidate
originals before attaching them. Use the master plus one or two relevant originals,
with current subject references first. Do not attach the whole collection or use
an inventory/contact sheet as the image-generation reference.

- **Mixed craft sheets:** sticker rhythm, rectangular vs die-cut shapes, scale,
  spacing and combinations of photography, games, code and character modules.
- **Character studies:** pose, crop, silhouette, turnaround, costume-detail
  layout and portrait framing. Their depicted characters are examples; use them
  for identity only when the brief explicitly requests that character.
- **Full illustrations:** depth, architectural framing, ribbons, focal lighting
  and the difference between a framed portrait and a full cinematic scene.
- **Photography and cards:** camera/editorial treatment, tabular information
  hierarchy and compact print labels. Captions and numbers are visual examples,
  not verified camera specifications, exposure advice or film characteristics.
- **Film labels:** compact lettering, information bands, cutlines and print
  texture. Their depicted film-stock names, colors and inscriptions are subject
  details only when explicitly requested; they do not redefine the house palette.
- **Gaming labels:** narrow strips, boot/save pairs, memory/controller badges,
  cover panels and cream outlines on dark backing. They do not automatically
  add PlayStation marks, game characters or blue accents to an unrelated brief.

`Light`, `Dark` and `Multicolor` below describe the reference, never the requested
output. Multicolor examples are useful for arrangement or requested subject
colors; translate their graphic structure into the selected system palette.
Keep the canonical master above these supporting references. Do not copy an
unrequested subject, logo, slogan, inscription or color scheme. No reference
silently establishes a rendering language or mode.
